The Process: Calcium Looping (CaL) Cycle

The leading high-temperature method is the Calcium Looping (CaL) cycle. This process uses a solid sorbent, typically Calcium Oxide (CaO), to capture CO₂ in one reactor and release it in a pure stream from another. The 850°C+ temperature occurs in the regeneration step.

The Challenge: Material Degradation

Natural materials like limestone (which becomes CaO) are cheap but not durable. The extreme heat of regeneration causes them to 'sinter'—their pores collapse, and they rapidly lose the ability to capture CO₂.

As shown, the capture capacity of natural limestone plummets after just a few cycles, making the process inefficient.

The Solution: Synthetic Sorbents

The solution is to engineer advanced sorbents. By 'doping' Calcium Oxide with a stable, heat-resistant material (like alumina or zirconia), a porous 'skeleton' is created that prevents sintering.

These advanced sorbents maintain their structure and performance, even after hundreds of cycles.

Performance Comparison: Natural vs. Synthetic

The difference in performance is stark. Advanced, doped sorbents vastly outperform natural limestone in the two most critical metrics: long-term stability and overall capture capacity.

Long-Term Cyclic Stability

The synthetic sorbent maintains a high, stable capture capacity, while the natural limestone's capacity collapses.

Effective Capture Capacity

While natural limestone starts high, its stable, usable capacity is very low. Doped sorbents are engineered for durability.

Key Industrial Applications

These robust materials are essential for decarbonizing "hard-to-abate" sectors where high-temperature heat is unavoidable and process emissions (not just energy) are a core problem.

🏭

Cement Production

Capturing CO₂ released directly from the chemical conversion of limestone (calcination), a primary source of emissions.

🔥

Steel Manufacturing

Integrating with blast furnaces to capture CO₂ from combustion and the iron ore reduction process.

⚡

Power & Hydrogen

Retrofitting power plants or enabling blue hydrogen production by capturing CO₂ from steam methane reforming.
